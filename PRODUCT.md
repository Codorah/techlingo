# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Francophone developers, computer science students and tech job seekers who already read some English but cannot yet *speak* tech in English: explaining their code, DevOps, web, mobile, networks or systems during an interview or a standup. They use the app on their phone in short sessions (5 to 7 minutes): on transit, before an interview, during a break.

## Product Purpose
TechLingo teaches technical English the way Duolingo teaches a language: one word a day (the spin wheel), spaced repetition so it sticks, quick practice games, and interview drills with model answers. Success means a user can answer "Can you explain what DevOps is?" out loud, in English, with the right vocabulary. Progress stays on the device; no account is required.

## Brand Personality
Pro, clear, efficient. The game mechanics of Duolingo (streaks, XP, celebrations, a mascot) delivered with the restraint of a professional tool. Encouraging without being childish; never guilt-tripping. Byte, the robot mascot, is friendly and curious, and appears at key moments only.

## Anti-references
- Emojis used as UI icons: every icon is a consistent stroke icon from one set.
- Website feel: pages that scroll like a document, no app shell, no native transitions.
- Bland card soup: identical white cards stacked with no hierarchy.
- Childish gamification: cartoon overload, loud colors everywhere, aggressive guilt notifications.
- Generic AI SaaS look: gradient text, glass cards, hero-metric tiles.

## Design Principles
1. **One primary action per screen.** The next step is always obvious and thumb-reachable.
2. **Earned delight.** Animation and celebration reward progress (a correct answer, a finished lesson); they never decorate idle screens.
3. **Show the English.** Technical terms, examples and pronunciation are the hero content; chrome stays quiet around them.
4. **Native, not web.** Bottom tab bar, screen transitions, press feedback, haptics and safe areas behave like an installed iOS/Android app.
5. **Pro over playful when they conflict.** Game mechanics stay, but tone and visuals favor credibility for a job-seeking audience.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.2 AA: 4.5:1 text contrast, 44px minimum touch targets, visible focus. Every animation has a reduced-motion alternative. Color is never the only signal for right/wrong (icon + text too). Light and dark themes.
